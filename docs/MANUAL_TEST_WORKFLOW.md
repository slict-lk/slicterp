# ACCOUNTING MODULE

## A1 — Accounting Dashboard

### What This Tab Does
The dashboard is a **read-only financial overview**. It shows you the big picture — money owed to you, money you owe others, recent activity, and quick links to every accounting function. Nothing can be created or deleted here.

### How to Use It
1. Click **Accounting** in the sidebar.
2. You land on the dashboard automatically.
3. Use the **module tab bar** at the top to filter financial data by department (All Modules, Spareparts, Vehicle Export, etc.).
4. The four stat cards update to show totals for the selected module.
5. The **Revenue by Module** chart shows the last 6 months of financial activity as stacked areas.
6. The **Module Breakdown** sidebar ranks departments by revenue with a percentage bar.
7. Scroll down to see **Recent GL Entries**, **Recent Invoices**, and **Recent Payments** as quick-preview lists.

### Action Buttons & Links
| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| Refresh | Reloads all numbers from the database |
| New Entry | Takes you to Journal Entries to post a manual adjustment |
| View All (Invoices row) | Takes you to the full Invoices list |
| View All (Payments row) | Takes you to the full Payments list |
| View All (GL Entries row) | Takes you to Journal Entries |
| Quick Action Grid links | Shortcut navigation to all accounting sub-pages |

### Test Checklist
- [ ] Page loads without error
- [ ] Switching module tabs updates the 4 stat cards
- [ ] "New Entry" navigates to Journal Entries
- [ ] "View All" links navigate to correct pages
- [ ] Chart renders (may show empty data if no transactions yet — that is normal)

### Expected Result on Fresh System
All stat cards show **LKR 0.00**. Charts show no data. Recent entries lists show empty states. This is correct — accounting data is generated by invoices and payments.

## A2 — Chart of Accounts

### What This Tab Does
The **Chart of Accounts (COA)** is the master list of every financial category your business uses — like a filing cabinet for money. Every transaction must be assigned to an account. You must set this up **before** creating journal entries or invoices.

### Account Types Explained
| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Asset** | Things the company owns | Cash, Bank, Accounts Receivable, Inventory |
| **Liability** | Things the company owes | Accounts Payable, Loans, Tax Payable |
| **Equity** | Owners' share | Capital, Retained Earnings |
| **Revenue** | Money earned | Sales Revenue, Service Income |
| **Expense** | Money spent | Cost of Goods Sold, Rent, Salaries |

### Normal Balance Rule
- **Debit** normal balance = Asset and Expense accounts grow when you debit them
- **Credit** normal balance = Liability, Equity, and Revenue accounts grow when you credit them

## A3 — Accounting Periods

### What This Tab Does
Accounting periods are **time windows** (usually one month) that control which dates transactions can be posted to. You must **open a period** before you can post invoices or journal entries to it. Once a period is **closed**, its data is locked for auditing — no changes can be made.

### Important Warning
> **Never close a period until you are certain all transactions for that month have been entered.** Closing is irreversible.

## A4 — Invoices

### What This Tab Does
Invoices are the **official money requests** your business sends to customers (Sales Invoices) or receives from suppliers (Purchase Bills). This is how accounts receivable (money owed to you) and accounts payable (money you owe) are tracked.

### Invoice Types Explained
| Type | Meaning | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales Invoice (AR)** | You are charging a customer | Workshop buys spare parts from you |
| **Purchase Bill (AP)** | Supplier is charging you | You buy parts from a supplier |

### Invoice Status Flow
```
DRAFT → (Finalize & Post) → OPEN → (Record Payment) → PAID
                                  → (Void) → VOID
```

## A6 — Journal Entries

### What This Tab Does
Journal entries are the **raw double-entry bookkeeping transactions** behind everything. Every invoice, every payment automatically creates journal entries in the background. This tab lets you see all of them, and also lets you manually post **adjusting entries** — such as recording depreciation, correcting errors, or posting opening balances.

### The Golden Rule of Double-Entry
Every entry must have **equal total debits and equal total credits**. The system will not let you post an unbalanced entry. The totals turn green when balanced and red when unbalanced.

## A7 — Bank Reconciliation

### What This Tab Does
Bank reconciliation is the process of **matching your internal payment records against your actual bank statement**. This catches errors, fraud, or missing entries. The left panel shows your bank statement; the right panel shows your GL transactions. You match them line by line.

### Current Status
The upload and auto-match features are partially implemented. File upload works (accepts CSV, PDF, XLS, XLSX, images). Parsing simulates 12 transactions. Full matching UI is in progress.

# INVENTORY MODULE

## I1 — Inventory Dashboard

### What This Tab Does
A **read-only snapshot** of your entire inventory health. This is the first screen you see when clicking Inventory. It shows total stock value, number of products tracked, how many items are running low, and how many orders are waiting to be received.

### How to Use It
1. Click **Inventory** in the sidebar.
2. Review the 4 KPI cards:
   - **Total Stock Value** — the total LKR value of everything in your warehouses
   - **Active Master Catalog Items** — "X active / Y total registered" shows how many products are enabled
   - **Low Stock Alerts** — items below their minimum threshold (should be 0 when stock is healthy)
   - **Pending Incoming Shipments** — purchase orders approved but not yet received
3. Scroll down to the **Recent Stock Activity** feed — shows last product movements with direction, timestamp, and quantity
4. The **Active Network** side card shows how many warehouses/sites are registered

> **Expected Result (after completing I2–I7):** Total Stock Value shows a positive LKR figure, Active Items shows count matching your catalog, Low Stock Alerts may show items if stock is low.

## I5 — Stock Movements

### What This Tab Does
The Stock Movements tab is a **read-only audit log** — it records every single time product quantities changed. You cannot create movements directly here; they are created automatically when you receive a purchase order or when sales are made. This tab is for **verification and investigation only**.

### What Each Column Means
| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Time & Date** | Exactly when the movement happened |
| **Direction** | ↑ UP arrow (green) = stock came IN · ↓ DOWN arrow (red) = stock went OUT |
| **Asset Profile** | Which product moved + which warehouse |
| **Event Type** | Why it moved: PURCHASE_RECEIPT, SALE, ADJUSTMENT, RETURN, etc. |
| **Integrations** | Which module triggered it (INVENTORY, SALES, POS, etc.) |
| **Reference** | PO number, invoice number, or manual reference |
| **Impact** | How many units moved + the unit cost at time of movement |

---

### How to Use Stock Movements for Investigation

**Scenario:** Customer says they returned a product 2 weeks ago but their stock credit hasn't been applied. How do you check?

1. Go to **Inventory → Movements**.
2. In the search bar, type the customer's product SKU (e.g., `BP-TOY-001`).
3. Look for movements of type **RETURN** in the results.
4. Check the **Reference** column for the original invoice number.
5. If no RETURN movement exists for that date, the return was never processed in the system — escalate to the sales team.

### Search Tips
- Search by **product name**: `Brake Pad`
- Search by **SKU**: `BP-TOY-001`
- Search by **reference**: `PO-2026-001`
- Search by **movement type**: `RETURN` or `RECEIPT`
- Search by **source module**: `SALES` or `POS`

> **Note:** No forms exist here. If data is incorrect, corrections are made through journal entries in accounting or inventory adjustments via purchase orders.

## I7 — Purchase Orders

### What This Tab Does
A **Purchase Order (PO)** is a formal document sent to a supplier requesting products at agreed prices. The workflow is:

```
Create PO (Draft) → Approve & Release → Supplier ships goods → Receive Items → Stock increases
```

POs track what you ordered, how much it costs, and what has arrived. When you click "Receive Items", the system automatically creates stock movement records and updates your inventory quantities.

### PO Status Explained
| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Draft** | PO created but not yet sent to supplier |
| **Pending Approval** | Submitted and waiting for manager sign-off |
| **Approved (Awaiting Receipt)** | Supplier has been notified, waiting for delivery |
| **Fulfilled / Received** | All items received, PO is complete |

## I9 — Reports (Intelligence & Reports)

### What This Tab Does
The Reports tab provides **three analytical views** of your inventory to help with purchasing decisions, financial valuation, and identifying your most / least valuable products.

### The Three Report Tabs

|---|---|

| **ABC Classifications** | Ranks products by value into Tier A (top 20%), B (middle), C (bottom) |
| **Stock Turnover** | How fast each product sells — high ratio = fast moving, low = slow moving |

### Step-by-Step: Reading Stock Turnover

1. Click the **Stock Turnover** tab.
2. The table shows:
   - **COGS (Last 30d)** — from sales and outward movements in the past 30 days
   - **Turnover Ratio** — higher is better (product sells quickly)

**How to read the ratio:**
- Ratio of **12x** = the product cycles through stock 12 times per year (monthly turnover)
- Ratio of **1x** = product takes a full year to sell through once
- Ratio near **0** = slow mover — consider whether to keep stocking it

> **Note:** Turnover data requires sales activity. Right after initial setup the COGS will be 0. Test this after processing several sales invoices.
